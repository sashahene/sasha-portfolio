    Eight-week build

    Two months is a solid amount of time for this, as long as you order parts this week, since shipping is the one thing you can't speed up. Here's how I'd split it up, roughly finishing around late November.

    1. Week 1, now. Order and prep

      Place the DigiKey order, then use the waiting time to install the Arduino software and watch a beginner soldering video or two. This is also a good week to push your survey out, since responses take time to come in.

    2. Week 2. Get it talking

      Make the XIAO blink its light, then connect the gas sensor on a breadboard and see readings on your laptop. Start the sensor's 24-hour warm-up as soon as it's wired, and practice soldering on one of your spare XIAOs.

    3. Week 3. Bluetooth

      Get the readings showing up on your phone with a free app like nRF Connect. Add the status LED while you're at it, since it's quick.

    4. Week 4. Collect real data

      This is the heart of the proof of concept. Test the sensor with different amounts of alcohol on a pad near the vents, record the readings in a spreadsheet, and make your graph. Note the room temperature and humidity each time.

    5. Week 5. Measure and design the case

      Measure your actual parts with a ruler or calipers, then design the case in Tinkercad or Onshape around those real sizes, with a little breathing room. Print the first version.

    6. Week 6. Fix the fit and assemble

      Your first print probably won't fit perfectly, so adjust and reprint. Then solder everything permanently and put it all together, including the skin seal and strap.

    7. Week 7. Test the finished bracelet

      Wear it, test it with the alcohol pad against the vents, and fix whatever breaks (something always does). Take photos and screen recordings for your presentation.

    8. Week 8. Presentation, plus buffer

      Build your slides: survey results, your data graph, both 3D models side by side, and your honest limitations. Any leftover time here is your safety net for delays.

    A couple of tips

    If you're working with a team, the case design (week 5) can start earlier in parallel using the sizes from the product pages, then get adjusted once the parts arrive. And if anything falls behind, protect week 4. The data graph is your most important evidence, so a working breadboard with good data beats a pretty case with no data.
